I wanted a putter. I also wanted to know whether I could carry something from an empty
Fusion sketch to a finished object with no team and no budget behind me.

## The design

I drew it from scratch in Fusion 360 and revised it several times. Every revision went
back to the manufacturers before I committed to it, which costs days on the calendar and
saves them everywhere else. The constraints that decide a design are the ones the person
cutting the metal tells you about.

![Fusion 360 model of the putter head](images/projects/putter/cad.png "The head model in Fusion 360.")

## Splitting the supply chain

Two vendors, on purpose:

- **Head form** from a manufacturer in China. Bulk material removal and the basic geometry.
- **Grooves and finish** from a local shop. Face detailing and final surface work.

@gallery images/projects/putter/head-raw.jpg, images/projects/putter/head-fin.jpg | Left: the head as it arrived. Right: after local grooving and finishing.

## Why that split

A factory overseas will take a lot of material off cheaply once the tooling exists, and it
will not care whether I order fifty or five thousand. It also stops caring somewhere in the
last ten percent, where tolerance and surface quality decide whether a club feels cheap in
your hands.

The local shop charges more per hour and answers the phone. When a groove came out wrong I
drove over and looked at it.

So I bought form from one and precision from the other.

## The part that matters

I ran this same split on a $600K hardware program at Dreamscape. Injection-molded shells,
custom PCBs and battery packs came from China; final assembly and electronic components I
sourced domestically. Five vendors, two countries, and BOM cost came down 30%.

Nobody asked me to reproduce it here. There was no program, no budget, and no one to
justify the decision to. It is where the constraints push you once you have done it before.

The putter is fine. The sourcing decision is why it is on this site.
