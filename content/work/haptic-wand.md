## The program

A **~$600K hardware program** covering the haptic wands, VR broom ride vehicles, and
supporting show elements for the store's immersive experiences.

The launch date was fixed by Warner Bros. and did not move. Everything else — scope,
tooling strategy, vendor selection — got managed around that one immovable constraint.

## The team

Two in-house engineers, one mechanical and one software. A third-party development
partner. Two to three design contractors. That was the whole program.

## Supply chain

Five vendors across two countries. Custom PCBs, battery packs, and the injection-molded
enclosure came from China; final assembly and electronic components were sourced
domestically, largely through Digi-Key.

**BOM cost came down 30%** across the program — roughly half from design refinement,
half from restructuring who made what and where.

## What I'd tell you about in an interview

Hard tooling for injection molding at a 200-unit run is an unusual call. The
tooling-cost-versus-volume math on that decision is the most interesting technical
conversation in this program, and it isn't something you can put in a bullet point.

The program ended when it was cancelled during the Warner/Discovery merger — unrelated
to how the product performed.
